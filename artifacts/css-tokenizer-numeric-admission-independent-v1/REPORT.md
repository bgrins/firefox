## Universal lexical substance

`NumericDigits` compares arithmetic digit classification with a literal digit list and recursive scanning with maximal take/drop-while selection. It proves consumed prefix, exact suffix, maximality and each recorded loop read bound. `NumericEntry` derives nonempty initial and post-sign input from actual digit/sign/dot dispatch, allowing a leading dot and zero integral digits. The reference shares elementary sign stripping, which is explicit and source-reviewed rather than presented as independent standard-library correctness.

`NumericParts` uses separate fraction/exponent grammar selectors based on a nonempty maximal digit prefix. Both short-circuit predicates are related to those selectors. A dot without a following digit and incomplete exponent remain unconsumed numerically. A later identifier unit may consume an e or e- suffix; the token proof accounts for that distinction.

`NumericLexical` composes exact cover, maximal parts, syntax type, explicit outer sign, positive length under entry and all consumed bytes being ASCII. `NumericReads` checks fraction/exponent guard offsets and shifted loop reads, including the exponent sign-body entry. The native digit loop stops at EOF rather than reading once beyond it; the empty recursive tail records no read. Initial sign inspection and first integral read have separate nonempty premises. These list bounds do not prove usize lookahead additions cannot overflow, nor replace the native index/heap contract.
