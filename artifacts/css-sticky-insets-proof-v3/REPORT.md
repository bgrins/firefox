## R3 correction

Root's full review of v2 accepted its existing results but held the complete native caller claim: `StickyEndpointCaller.complete_machine_axis_caller` projected a `Frame2` before bounding arithmetic. The actual selected-axis range helper calls the complete two-coordinate `ComputeStickyLimits`, and `ComputeStickyDisplacement` subtracts a complete `nsPoint` before selecting its component. The other coordinate can overflow even when the isolated-axis premises hold. Root's three original counterexamples, including ordinary-coordinate-bounded inset inputs, are copied byte-for-byte under review-v2 and imported as RootCrossAxis.

`StickyFullEndpoint` selects the ancestor while retaining the full Frame2. Its entry guard follows no container, no eligible ancestor, multiple ancestors, missing offsets and selected-axis all-auto returns. Only the requested axis controls entering the complete core, but that core produces both coordinate results and requires both executed-expression domains. Selected range arithmetic consumes the selected component of this full core; it is not supplied a separately correct final range. After nonempty ranges, a delayed shift computation evaluates both point subtractions before selecting the requested component. The delay preserves the native early return: no shift bounds are imposed on absent ranges.

`complete_entry_refines` proves equality of the complete modeled observation: both computed core coordinates when reached, both shift coordinates when reached, and the selected displacement/optional defaults. `scan_projects` connects the actual full-frame scan to the earlier projected ancestor reference. `full_displacement_projection` joins the selected displacement to the existing native-shaped range model. `complete_two_axis_caller` combines this complete entry result with the existing independent normalization/range/filtered-ancestor and strict Earliest/Latest reference. Its entry premise is EntrySafe2 over the unprojected frames. The old complete_machine_axis_caller is retained unchanged as a historical isolated-axis intermediate and is not the new native-domain acceptance theorem.

CoreSafe and ShiftSafe bound expression trees and their executed branches, not a desired final observation. The domain remains conservative: the existing core domain includes representability of reconstructed endpoints as well as temporary writes. It is not claimed to be a maximal set of all safe C++ inputs. The early-return guards do not impose arithmetic conditions on a helper that is never entered. Signed32 wrapping is a diagnostic model only; no C++ signed-overflow execution is admitted.
