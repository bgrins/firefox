Why the reference is the intended rounding: let r be nearest-even integral rounding of finite x. The mathematical error x−r lies in [−1/2,1/2]. Away from a tie, r is the unique nearest integer. At the lower tie x=r−1/2, r is already the larger integer. At the upper tie x=r+1/2, r+1 is the larger integer, so only positive-half residual requires correction. The residual subtraction is exact: r=0 gives x directly; otherwise x and r have the same sign and their magnitudes satisfy the Sterbenz factor-of-two condition (the zero/tie boundary is handled separately). A half-integer binary32 input has magnitude below 2^23, so the corrected integer is also representable in binary32. Three additional symbolic mismatch queries returned UNSAT: the binary32 residual equals the promoted binary64 subtraction; the tie correction equals promoted addition of one; and both rounded outputs are always finite and integral. These complement, rather than replace, the stated mathematical exactness argument.

The separate `NearestInterval.lean` theorem kernel-checks the abstract integer/rational bridge: for d>0 and 2dr−d ≤ 2n ≤ 2dr+d, `(2n+d)/(2d)` equals r+1 exactly at the upper endpoint, and r otherwise. Lean's positive-divisor integer division is floor. The proof does not import floating-point semantics or certify Z3. Linking IEEE nearest-even/residual operations to this interval remains the explicit argument above. Two false half-tie controls are rejected.
