A mathematical justification for widening is separate from that evidence. For binary32 x with 1/2 ≤ |x| ≤ 2^31, x+1/2 needs at most 33 significant binary digits: at small exponents the aligned half adds at most one significand bit, and at large exponents the half supplies one fractional bit below a bounded integer. Binary64 can represent that sum exactly. For |x|<1/2, the result must be zero; widening cannot round the closest binary32 predecessor of 1/2 up to the integer boundary. Values outside the signed32 interval saturate under the existing cast. This argument assumes ordinary IEEE operations; it is not kernel-checked or a compiler-refinement theorem. NaN/infinity normalization and cast behavior are retained structurally rather than added to the finite-input test domain.
