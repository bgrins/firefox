The claim is narrowly that unforced text inside `white-space: nowrap` must not acquire soft line breaks merely because its break policy is break-all or anywhere, or because its text is split across adjacent/nested inline text runs.

The [CSS Text 4 nowrap rule](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-text-4/#valdef-text-wrap-mode-nowrap) in the 26 October 2026 editor's draft confines breaks to forced breaks and makes excess text overflow. The fixture's `ABC D` contains no forced break. Its nowrap line-count oracle follows directly; Ahem calibration and min-content checks make the incorrect soft opportunities observable. The separately wrapping container retains an allowed break before the nowrap inline; normal wrapping controls deliberately retain internal breaks. **REACHED** for this fixture's nowrap oracle, not a proof for all CSS text processing.

The implementation correspondence is the documented `nsLineBreaker::BREAK_SUPPRESS_INITIAL` and `BREAK_SUPPRESS_INSIDE` contract. Current `BuildTextRunsScanner::ScanFrame` derives these from wrapping policy. Existing `FlushCurrentWord` distinguishes chunk-initial and interior positions; the proposed two AppendText callback masks apply the same distinction to immediate output. `nsTextFrame::ShouldSuppressLineBreak` is not an alternative ordinary-nowrap suppression mechanism (its additional suppression concerns ruby).
